José Rizal: Life & Works

Childhood of José Rizal

Discover the birth, family, early education, and childhood experiences that helped shape the life of the Philippine national hero.

Early life · 1861–1872

The beginnings of a remarkable life

José Protacio Rizal was born in Calamba, Laguna, on June 19, 1861. He was the seventh child of Francisco Mercado Rizal and Teodora Alonso Realonda. He grew up in a family that valued education, reading, faith, and responsibility.

His earliest years were shaped by his mother's lessons, his father's support for his studies, and the guidance of relatives and tutors. Even as a child, Rizal showed an interest in language, literature, drawing, and the natural world. The events of his childhood also exposed him to personal loss and the political realities of Spanish colonial society.

June 19, 1861

The Birth of Rizal

Birthplace

Calamba, Laguna

José P. Rizal, the seventh child of the Rizal family, was born on Wednesday, June 19, 1861, in Calamba, in the province of Laguna, Philippines. According to the account provided in the source material, he was born between 11:00 p.m. and midnight.

A difficult delivery

His Mother's Ordeal

Rizal's mother, Teodora Alonso Realonda, reportedly experienced a difficult delivery. The source account explains that the size of the baby's head made the birth especially dangerous for her.

June 22, 1861

Baptism in Calamba

Three days after his birth, Rizal was baptized at the Catholic Church in Calamba by the parish priest, Father Rufino Collantes. His godfather was Father Pedro Casañas, described in the source material as a close friend of the Rizal family.

A prediction

The Parish Priest's Remark

The account relates that Father Collantes was struck by the size of the infant's head and encouraged the family to take good care of him, predicting that he would someday become a great man.

“Take good care of this child, for someday he will become a great man.”

— Words attributed to Father Rufino Collantes in the supplied account

Rizal's birth and baptism are commonly recorded as June 19 and June 22, 1861, respectively. The wording of historical anecdotes and quotations may differ among biographies and retellings.

Family identity

Understanding Rizal's Complete Name

Rizal's name appears in historical sources in slightly different forms, including José Protacio Rizal Mercado y Alonso Realonda. The supplied material also presents the form “Jose Protacio Mercado Rizal Alonzo y Realonda.” The explanations below follow the naming information in the supplied account.

José Protacio

José was chosen by his mother in honor of Saint Joseph, whose feast day is celebrated on March 19. The supplied material explains that the first two given names were associated with saints.

Mercado

The surname Mercado was associated with Rizal's Chinese ancestor, Domingo Lam-co. The Mercado name continued to be used by members of the family even after they adopted Rizal as an additional surname.

Rizal

According to the supplied account, the family adopted the surname Rizal during the 1840s, following the implementation of the Clavería decree concerning surnames.

Alonso and Realonda

Alonso was the family name associated with Rizal's mother, Teodora Alonso. Realonda was also used in the family's maternal naming tradition, as described in the supplied source.

In Spanish naming conventions, “y” means “and.” It can appear between family names in older records. Historical documents and modern references do not always spell or arrange Rizal's complete name identically.

Home and family

The People Who Influenced His Childhood

Teodora Alonso Realonda

Rizal's mother introduced him to the alphabet, reading, writing, prayer, and literature. Her early instruction helped establish the foundation for his later education and literary interests.

Francisco Mercado Rizal

Rizal's father supported his son's education and arranged lessons beyond those available at home. Among the tutors he hired was Leon Monroy, who introduced the young Rizal to the rudiments of Latin.

Uncle Manuel Alberto

The supplied account describes how Uncle Manuel Alberto encouraged Rizal's physical development and appreciation of the outdoors. He helped the young boy develop an admiration for nature.

Uncle Gregorio

A scholar, Uncle Gregorio encouraged Rizal to work carefully, think independently, and develop a love of learning. His advice emphasized diligence, thoroughness, and the importance of forming clear mental images of what one studies.

1862–1872

Childhood Timeline

The following timeline organizes the important dates and childhood events provided in the supplied material.

  1. September 28, 1862

    The Burning of the Parish Records

    The parochial church of Calamba and its canonical books, including the book containing Rizal's baptismal record, were reportedly burned.

  2. 1864

    Learning the Alphabet

    At barely three years old, Rizal learned the alphabet from his mother, beginning his early education at home.

  3. 1865

    The Death of Concepción

    Rizal's younger sister Concepción, the eighth child in the family, died at the age of three. The account recalls that Rizal shed what he remembered as his first genuine tears during this period of family grief.

  4. 1865–1867

    Reading, Writing, and Latin

    His mother continued teaching him to read and write. His father hired Leon Monroy, a classmate, to teach him the rudiments of Latin. Monroy tutored Rizal for approximately five months before his death.

  5. June 6, 1868

    Pilgrimage to Antipolo

    Rizal traveled with his father to Antipolo to fulfill a vow made by his mother. She had promised to bring her child to the Shrine of the Virgin of Antipolo if both survived the difficult delivery.

    From Antipolo, Rizal and his father proceeded to Manila to visit his sister Saturnina, who was studying at La Concordia College in Santa Ana.

  6. 1869 · Age eight

    The Poem “Sa Aking Mga Kabata”

    The supplied material attributes the poem “Sa Aking Mga Kabata” to Rizal at the age of eight. Written in Tagalog, it is associated with the theme of love for one's language.

    Attribution of this poem to Rizal has been questioned by some scholars; the traditional attribution should therefore be distinguished from a conclusively verified authorship.

  7. 1870

    Studies in Biñan, Laguna

    Rizal's older brother Paciano brought him to Biñan, where he studied under Justiniano Aquino Cruz. His lessons included Latin and Spanish. He also received instruction in painting from an older painter named Juancho Carrera, according to the supplied account.

  8. December 17, 1870

    Return to Calamba

    After completing his studies in Biñan, Rizal returned to Calamba aboard the motorboat Talim. His parents planned for him to continue his education in Manila.

  9. 1871

    His Mother's Imprisonment

    Teodora Alonso was imprisoned in Santa Cruz, Laguna, following an accusation that she had poisoned the wife of her cousin José Alberto. The case became a painful episode in the family's history.

  10. 1872

    The Cavite Mutiny and the GOMBURZA

    Rizal became aware of the term filibustero, which his father reportedly forbade the family to utter. The term was associated with the political tension following the Cavite Mutiny of January 20, 1872, and the execution of Fathers Mariano Gómez, José Burgos, and Jacinto Zamora on February 17, 1872.

    The executions deeply affected Filipino society and became an important part of the historical background to Rizal's later political awareness.

The foundation of learning

Rizal's Early Education

At home

Learning from His Mother

Rizal's earliest lessons came from his mother, who taught him letters, reading, writing, and religious instruction. The home environment encouraged his curiosity and provided his first contact with books and stories.

Private tutoring

Lessons with Leon Monroy

Leon Monroy introduced Rizal to Latin. Although their lessons were cut short by Monroy's death, they formed part of Rizal's early preparation for more formal schooling.

Biñan, Laguna

Formal Lessons

Under Justiniano Aquino Cruz, Rizal studied Latin and Spanish. His time in Biñan also included exposure to painting, expanding his interests beyond academic subjects.

Personal development

Nature, Art, and Independent Thought

Family members encouraged Rizal to observe the natural world, practice his skills carefully, and think independently. These influences complemented his formal lessons and supported his growing intellectual interests.

Rizal's childhood education began in the family home and continued through private tutoring and schooling in Biñan. His early experiences laid groundwork for his later studies in Manila and abroad.
