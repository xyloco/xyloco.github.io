The Life & Works of José Rizal

The Educational Journey of Dr. José Rizal

From his first lessons at home to his advanced medical training in Europe, Rizal's pursuit of knowledge shaped his character, strengthened his talents, and helped prepare him to serve his country.

A Lifelong Pursuit of Knowledge

Dr. José Rizal's education began long before he entered a formal school. His mother, Teodora Alonso, introduced him to reading, prayer, and literature, nurturing his curiosity and appreciation for learning. His father also arranged private lessons, including early instruction in Latin.

As Rizal continued his studies, he developed abilities in languages, literature, science, medicine, and the arts. His education took him from Laguna and Manila to Spain, France, and Germany. These experiences broadened his understanding of the world and brought him into contact with people and ideas that influenced his views on Philippine society.

For Rizal, education became more than a means of personal achievement. It helped him understand the injustices experienced by Filipinos and gave him tools to express his ideas, challenge abuses, and advocate for meaningful reform.

"Rizal's journey demonstrates how education can develop not only individual talent, but also a deeper sense of responsibility toward society."
A Chronological Account

From Home to Europe

Explore the schools, teachers, achievements, and experiences that shaped Rizal's intellectual development.

Early Childhood

His First Teachers at Home

Rizal's educational journey began under the guidance of his mother, Teodora Alonso, who taught him to read, pray, and appreciate stories and literature. His father also arranged private lessons, including instruction in Latin.

These early experiences encouraged Rizal's curiosity, discipline, and interest in reading, writing, and the arts.

Around 1870–1871

Studies in Biñan, Laguna

Rizal attended school in Biñan under Maestro Justiniano Aquino Cruz. His studies included Latin, Spanish, and other academic subjects. His teacher was known for strict discipline and recognized Rizal's ability to learn.

His experience in Biñan helped prepare him for further education in Manila.

Historical note: Sources differ on whether Rizal began schooling in Biñan in 1870 or 1871. The National Historical Commission of the Philippines identifies 1871, while some biographies give 1870.
June 1872

Entrance Examinations in Manila

On June 10, 1872, Rizal took entrance examinations at Colegio de San Juan de Letran. The examinations included Christian doctrine, arithmetic, and reading.

He passed and was admitted to Ateneo Municipal de Manila on June 26, 1872.

Important clarification: Rizal took the entrance examinations at Letran, but he attended Ateneo Municipal de Manila as a student.
1872–1877

Ateneo Municipal de Manila

Managed by the Jesuits, Ateneo provided Rizal with instruction in languages, literature, mathematics, philosophy, and science. He also cultivated his abilities in poetry, drawing, painting, and sculpture.

Father José Bech was his first teacher at Ateneo, while Father Francisco de Paula Sánchez became one of his favorite teachers.

On March 23, 1877, Rizal graduated with a Bachelor of Arts degree and received the distinction sobresaliente, meaning excellent or outstanding. He also pursued surveying and land appraisal at Ateneo.

1877–1882

University of Santo Tomas

In 1877, Rizal enrolled at the University of Santo Tomas (UST), initially studying Philosophy and Letters. In 1878, he pursued medicine, partly because he wanted to help his mother with her eyesight.

He continued writing poetry during this period. In 1879, his poem A la Juventud Filipina (To the Filipino Youth) won a literary prize.

Rizal did not complete his medical degree at UST. In May 1882, he left the Philippines to continue his education in Spain.

1882–1885

Universidad Central de Madrid

In November 1882, Rizal enrolled at the Universidad Central de Madrid, where he studied Medicine and Philosophy and Letters.

He obtained his Licentiate in Medicine on June 21, 1884, and completed his studies in Philosophy and Letters in 1885.

His years in Spain also brought him into contact with Filipino reformists who advocated greater equality and reforms under Spanish colonial rule.

1885–1886

Medical Training in Paris

After studying in Madrid, Rizal continued his medical training in Paris in 1885. He trained under Louis de Wecker, an ophthalmologist, developing his knowledge of eye care.

This training was especially relevant to his desire to help people with eye conditions, including his mother.

1886

Further Studies in Heidelberg, Germany

Rizal continued his ophthalmological training in Heidelberg under Otto Becker. His time in Germany further developed his scientific knowledge and broadened his cultural understanding.

His international experiences strengthened the intellectual foundation he would bring to his writings and his efforts to promote the welfare of Filipinos.

Beyond the Classroom

What Education Gave Rizal

Rizal's education developed several abilities that influenced his work as a physician, writer, and advocate for the Filipino people.

01

Scientific Knowledge

His medical studies and ophthalmological training equipped him with scientific knowledge and practical skills to help people, particularly those with eye conditions.

02

Literary Excellence

His love of reading and writing developed into poetry, essays, and novels through which he communicated his ideas and criticized injustices.

03

Critical Thinking

His exposure to different subjects, cultures, and political ideas helped him examine social problems and consider ways to improve the conditions of Filipinos.

04

Artistic Expression

Rizal's interests in drawing, painting, sculpture, and literature allowed him to express his observations and ideas through different forms of art.

05

International Perspective

Studying in Europe exposed Rizal to different educational systems, cultures, and reform movements, expanding his understanding of society and government.

06

Service to Others

Rizal's education became a means of helping others, expressing concern for his country, and advocating for the dignity and welfare of the Filipino people.

A Student's Perspective

Reflection on Rizal's Kabayanihan

How Rizal's life, education, and sacrifices continue to inspire Filipino students today.

Dr. José Rizal is a great example of a Filipino hero because he used his knowledge, talents, and courage to fight for the rights of his fellow Filipinos. He did not rely on weapons to become a hero. Instead, he used his knowledge to expose the abuses and injustices that his fellow Filipinos experienced during the Spanish colonial period. He showed that we do not actually need weapons to fight for ourselves or for our freedom. Words and education can also be powerful tools for change and justice.

As a second-year college student, I learned from Dr. José Rizal that education should not only be used to achieve personal success. We can also use our knowledge and ideas to understand the problems in our community and help our fellow Filipinos. His life reminds me that standing up for ourselves and other people requires courage. It can be difficult, and it may put us in a dangerous position, but we need to be brave and willing to make sacrifices for the betterment of our lives.

Rizal's sacrifice also teaches me to appreciate the freedom and opportunities we have today. He dedicated much of his life to fighting for the dignity and welfare of Filipinos, even though his actions placed him in danger. Without him and our other brave heroes, our country's history might have unfolded differently. His execution on December 30, 1896, reminds us of the great sacrifices made by people who fought for our country.

I believe that we can honor Rizal by being responsible students, respecting other people, respecting our history, learning from the past, visiting historical places to pay our respects, and using our knowledge to help improve our community. We do not have to become famous to make a difference, and we do not need weapons to stand up for justice. We can use our knowledge to do so. For me, true kabayanihan begins with loving and respecting our country and showing that love through our everyday actions.

Camacho, Irish Shayne C.
